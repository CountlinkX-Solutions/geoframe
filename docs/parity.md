# GeoPandas / GeoPolars (dev) / GeoFrame parity matrix

Sources: GeoPandas stable API reference (geoseries, geodataframe, io, tools, sindex, testing pages all fetched OK; the
reference index page was not needed). GeoPolars `dev` snapshot (`docs/Roadmap.md`, `docs/Tier per expression.md`,
`geopolars/geopolars/geo/*.py`, `expr/geo.py`). GeoFrame = `main` (`lib/**`, `native/geoframe_core`).

Legend: ✅ done | 🟡 partial | ❌ missing | ⛔ out of scope.
GeoPolars column: `yes` = exposed in the Python API on `dev`; `no`/`roadmap` = absent (the Roadmap lists binary predicates,
set ops, interpolate/project, line joining etc. as unchecked). GeoFrame names are `GeoFrame.GeoSeries.*` unless noted.
GeoFrame backend today: `geo` 0.33, `geoarrow-*` 0.9, `geoparquet` 0.9, `proj4rs`, `flatgeobuf`, `geozero`, `shapefile` 0.9, `rusqlite` 0.40 with bundled SQLite (the only C code, compiled by `cc`), `zip`, `geo-buf`-style buffer. See `native/geoframe_core/Cargo.toml` for the exact versions.
Note: GeoFrame has no GeoDataFrame-level delegation of GeoSeries methods other than area/length/bounds/centroid
(users call `GeoSeries` on `gdf.geometry`); this is counted as ✅ for the series member and noted once in section 12.

## Reference testing

Since batch V every operation GeoFrame shares with GeoPandas is checked against the real library:
`test/reference/generate.py` (dev-only; GeoPandas 1.2.0, Shapely 2.2.0, GEOS 3.14.1, pyproj 3.8.0) computes
the results for a fixed corpus and commits them as JSON in `test/reference/fixtures/`;
`test/geo_frame/reference_test.exs` compares GeoFrame with them in CI without Python (18 864 cases
across 114 operations, including sjoin, sjoin_nearest, dissolve and union_all on small frames and, since batch B,
`overlay` (all five `how`s, with and without `keep_geom_type`, on polygon, line and point frames), `clip`, `clip_by_rect`,
`explode` and `intersection_all`; since batch E also concave hull, bounding shapes, inscribed circle, Delaunay,
constrained Delaunay, Voronoi and `make_grid`; since batch I1 also `is_simple`, `normalize`, `set_precision`,
`get_precision`, `to_json` and `__geo_interface__`; since batch I2 also `offset_curve`, `snap`, `shared_paths`,
`minimum_clearance`, `minimum_clearance_line`, `polygonize` and `build_area`; since batch I3 also `is_valid_coverage`,
`invalid_coverage_edges` and `simplify_coverage`; since Z1 also `has_z`, `has_m`, `z`, `m`, `force_2d`, `force_3d` and Z/M WKB and WKT; since Z2 the Z/M behaviour of the affine functions, `to_crs`, `reverse`, `normalize`, `boundary`, `exterior`, `get_geometry`, `simplify`, `set_precision` and `snap`; Z5 adds `get_coordinates(include_z:, include_m:)` and `from_xy(z:)` in the standalone `zm_coords.json`). 18 296 cases agree, 730 are documented deviations (most of them the
null/empty class) and none is unexplained (`REFERENCE_REPORT=1 mix test test/geo_frame/reference_test.exs` prints the table per operation). Since batch G1 the I/O fixtures are separate: 23 small Feather, GeoParquet and Arrow
IPC files written by GeoPandas 1.2 / pyarrow 26 (`test/reference/fixtures/io/`, generated by `generate_io.py`) are read in CI by
`test/geo_frame/parity_g1_test.exs`, and `scripts/crosscheck_io.sh` (dev-only) checks the opposite direction: 100 files written
by GeoFrame (every format, including Z, M and ZM files checked with their ordinates, null geometries and multi-geometry GeoParquet, Feather and Arrow files) read correctly in GeoPandas, and 23 Z/M files (`test/reference/fixtures/io/zm/`) are read in CI by `test/geo_frame/zm_io_test.exs`. Known differences
are listed in `test/reference/known_deviations.exs` and the suite asserts that each one still deviates. See
`test/reference/README.md` for the tolerances and how to regenerate. Documented deviations:

- **Null and empty inputs.** GeoFrame returns `nil` where GeoPandas returns `False`, `0`, `NaN` or an empty
  geometry (`area`, predicates, `centroid`, `envelope`, `bounds`, ...); `count_geometries` of an empty atomic
  geometry is 0 (Shapely: 1). `POINT EMPTY` cannot be stored in `geo` types.
- **Non-finite results.** A measure that overflows or is undefined (`area`, `length`, `distance`, `hausdorff_distance`,
  `frechet_distance`, `project`, `bounds`, `total_bounds`, `x`, `y`, `z`, `m`, geodesic and minimum-bounding measures)
  is `:infinity`, `:neg_infinity` or `:nan` in the Explorer `f64` series, as GeoPandas gives `inf`/`nan` (issue #60);
  the NIF used to raise `ArgumentError` there. Overflow inside a distance gives `:infinity` as GEOS does. GeoFrame
  can differ where GEOS overflows an intermediate (a 1e200 square: `length` is finite here, `inf` in GEOS).
- **Invalid input.** Predicates and `relate` on a self-intersecting bowtie polygon follow geo's relate and differ
  from GEOS in a few pairs. Valid inputs agree in every case.
- **Zero-area polygons and degenerate lines.** A polygon ring whose vertices are all collinear is invalid, as in
  GEOS, and `make_valid` collapses it to its linework (`MULTILINESTRING` noded at every vertex, a `LINESTRING` for
  two distinct points, a `POINT` for one; a collapsed hole is dropped and kept as a line beside the polygon, other
  polygons of a multipolygon stay, as a `GEOMETRYCOLLECTION`). Not repaired, and left unchanged: a collapsed shell
  that has holes (GEOS builds a polygon from the holes), and a geometry collection holding such a polygon.
  Lines that overlap across parts are not merged. `is_ring` is false for the two-point closed line
  `LINESTRING (1 1, 1 1)`; `is_valid_reason` uses geo's wording, not GEOS' text (for a collinear ring it gives
  GEOS' `Self-intersection[x y]` / `Too few points in geometry component[x y]`).
- **`envelope` of a horizontal/vertical line.** GeoFrame returns a line; GEOS 3.14 returns a zero-area polygon with
  repeated vertices.
- **`buffer` mitre/bevel joins** on the acute corner of a closed line differ between geo-buf and GEOS; round caps
  and joins, flat and square caps on open lines agree (area within 2 %).
- **`line_merge` of a polygon** gives an empty collection (GEOS merges its boundary).
- **Set operations.** Every point/line/polygon combination agrees with GEOS (batch B removed the 302 series
  deviations and the mixed-family `dissolve` one). Remaining: `GeometryCollection` input gives `nil` for `union`,
  `difference` and `symmetric_difference` (storage flattens nested collections). Results agree within 1e-6, not
  bit-exactly: `geo`'s boolean operations snap intersection points to an integer grid (about 1e-8 of the extent).
- **Batch E.** Sixteen documented cases, all on degenerate or tied input: the concave hull of the symmetric
  `e_ring` point set (equal edge lengths erode in another order); `minimum_rotated_rectangle` of triangles and
  symmetric shapes (several equal-area minimal rectangles); `minimum_bounding_circle` of `LINESTRING (1 1, 1 1)`
  (GEOS: `POLYGON EMPTY`); `maximum_inscribed_circle` of an invalid bowtie; vertex snapping with a `tolerance` on
  clustered input (GEOS snaps against the endpoints of the triangle edge a new vertex lands on, GeoFrame against
  every kept vertex in lexicographic order); Voronoi sides of near-cocircular rounded points. Triangle sets are
  compared exactly, falling back to "same count, same union" because cocircular points have several valid
  triangulations; `maximum_inscribed_circle` is a search, so its radius is compared within the tolerance.
- **Batch I1.** `set_precision` on an invalid multipolygon (parts that overlap or lie inside each other): GEOS's
  overlay noder decides from edge contact after snapping (a part whose edges meet no other part's is kept, even in an
  invalid result; parts whose edges coincide are merged; parts whose edges cross make GEOS raise a `TopologyException`,
  "side location conflict"), GeoFrame merges parts that overlap after snapping and never raises (six documented cases; valid input agrees,
  including the half-up rounding of negative numbers). `to_json` compares as decoded JSON (key order and spacing
  are not compared); a null or empty geometry has `"bbox": null` in both.
- **Batch I2.** Fifty-eight documented cases on the new operations, all on awkward input. `offset_curve` (35): the
  outer side of a closed `LineString` with a bevel or mitre join (GEOS keeps the closing bevel or drops the two
  pieces next to the start because it reads the curve off the buffer outline, whose round caps meet there; 24 cases),
  a polygon ring that is offset exactly onto the medial axis (2), and mitre or bevel joins around segments shorter than
  the distance, where GEOS cuts against the bevel-shaped buffer and GeoFrame against the disc around each vertex (9).
  The curve is compared by length and Hausdorff distance (within 1e-6); vertices GEOS adds by simplifying its input
  are not reproduced. `snap` (21): several snap points competing for one vertex or segment, where the order GEOS
  applies them in is undocumented (the port agrees in about 99 % of random cases). `polygonize` and `build_area` (1
  each): lon/lat input whose overlapping polygons are merged with geo's overlay, which snaps to a grid of about
  1e-9 degrees. `shared_paths`, `minimum_clearance` and `minimum_clearance_line` agree everywhere except the
  null/empty class; the clearance line compares equal when only an equally close pair differs.
- **Batch I3.** Three documented cases and a handful of intended differences. `invalid_coverage_edges` over a series
  with a null row (3 cases): GeoPandas 1.2 drops the null geometry before calling GEOS and pads the result with `NaN` at
  the end, so every result after the null moves up one row; GeoFrame keeps each result in its row and gives `nil`
  for the null one. Where Shapely raises (`simplify_coverage` over a null or a non-polygon row) GeoFrame returns
  `nil` for that row and simplifies the rest; `is_valid_coverage` and `invalid_coverage_edges` ignore non-polygons as
  Shapely does. A non-finite `gap_width` or `tolerance` is an `ArgumentError` (Shapely accepts `NaN` and `inf` silently), a
  row with a `NaN` or infinite coordinate is `nil`, and the work is capped (see [Resource limits](#resource-limits)). `simplify_coverage` and
  the validators were reconstructed from the published GEOS/JTS behaviour and then fitted to Shapely by black-box
  fuzzing (the `gap_width` rule, the vertex-in-neighbour test, the way a ring is rebuilt from its edges: the first
  edge as stored, so a ring may start at another node and come out reversed): 8,900 random validator cases,
  10,800 random coverages, 280 hand-built ones and 12,402 overlapping inputs agree vertex for vertex, as do the 805
  committed fixture cases. Structures the generators do not make (for instance one free ring shared by three levels
  of nesting) are untested.
- **Tolerances.** `to_crs` against PROJ: within 1e-6 projected units (1e-9 degrees back), including the Florida
  East (ftUS) transverse Mercator. Geodesic area, length and point distance: within 1e-6 of `pyproj.Geod`.

Fixed thanks to the suite: `distance`/`dwithin` returned `0.0`/`true` (or `f64::MAX` for an empty collection) for
empty inputs instead of `nil`; `remove_repeated_points` reduced a collapsed line to a one-point `LINESTRING`
(GEOS keeps two points).

## Datum shifts and grids

Issue #62. `to_crs` moves a datum that is not WGS 84 the way PROJ does when it has no network:
a grid if one is installed, else a Helmert (`+towgs84`) set. GeoFrame never downloads a grid.

* **Helmert by area of use (default).** Without a grid PROJ runs the best operation whose area
  of use holds the point, and a ballpark transformation (no shift) where none does and its list
  has a ballpark entry. GeoFrame has the same list per datum: `datum_helmert.rs`, generated from
  the PROJ database by `test/reference/generate_crs_datum.py helmert_table` (`TransformerGroup`
  of every geographic 2D EPSG CRS to WGS 84: 606 CRSs, 307 distinct lists, 833 operations, about
  135 KB). Operations are tried in this order: not for an offshore area first (an area name that
  says "offshore" before it says "onshore", whatever the accuracies: "Norway - offshore north of
  62°N. Also Svalbard - onshore and offshore." is offshore, "Nigeria - onshore and offshore." is
  not), then known accuracy, best first, then smaller area, then PROJ's own order. Against
  `Transformer.get_last_used_operation` at about 5 500 sampled points of every CRS with two or
  more operations this picks PROJ's operation for 95.4 % (the former rule, "offshore without
  onshore", 95.3 %, but it left the onshore Norwegian and Finnish Lapland on an offshore
  operation: Tromso, Rovaniemi); the remaining misses are offshore areas with several
  operations of the same accuracy. Molodensky-Badekas operations are folded into an equivalent
  Helmert set (pivot in the translation), inverse steps are inverted and several Helmert steps
  are composed into one (Garoua, Kousseri, Mhast (offshore): two small-angle steps, 1e-11
  relative). Four more kinds of operation:
  * a **geographic offset** (`geogoffset`: Anguilla 1957, 18" and 4.4" of latitude and longitude;
    the Greek datum, an offset of -5.86" and 0.28" followed by a translation on GRS80): the
    coordinates go from the CRS to geographic ones on its ellipsoid, are moved, and are read as
    WGS 84 (or GRS80 with the translation) before going to the target, in both directions
    (`reproject.rs`, `via_wgs84`);
  * a **null operation** (`noop`) is the same offset with zeros: PROJ runs it first for GDA94 and
    GDA2020 (EPSG "to WGS 84 (1)", accuracy 3 m, the same accuracy as the 1.7 m Helmert step the
    table used to run instead), and the table holds one for 162 CRSs (18 lists): GDA94 and its 42
    MGA, Vicgrid and Albers CRSs, GDA2020 and its 41, CR-SIRGAS, Anguilla 1957, Madrid 1870 and
    others, which now answer like pyproj (1.2 to 1.8 m before for the Australian ones). A
    geocentric step between two ellipsoids with no Helmert between (GSK-2011) is a zero
    `+towgs84`;
  * an **exact Helmert** (`helmert exact`, Saba, Sint Eustatius, Bonaire: rotations of 2e-3 rad,
    where the small-angle `+towgs84` formula of `proj4rs` is 9 m off): the exact rotation matrix
    on geocentric coordinates, in both directions (`datum.rs`, `ExactHelmert`);
  * an operation PROJ runs although the table of `TransformerGroup` lists only a ballpark: RD/83
    and PD/83 have a direct operation that needs a grid, and PROJ then composes the Helmert to
    ETRS89 with the null step to WGS 84 (182 m before).

  When no operation holds the point, PROJ keeps its ballpark if its list has one (ED50, AGD66) and
  otherwise runs the first operation of its list: a geographic CRS with no ballpark entry has that
  operation last in the table, with the whole world as its area (Minna at 40E 10N, DHDN in the
  North Sea), and a datum with one operation applies it everywhere (RD/83, Saba). For a projected
  CRS PROJ first keeps the operations that meet the area of use of that CRS and orders them by it
  (ED50 / UTM zone 30N at 8W 42N runs the Spanish north-west operation, not the Portuguese one;
  Minna / UTM zone 31N runs the 7 m offshore operation, not the 15 m onshore and offshore one):
  GeoFrame does not know that area, so it orders by the geographic CRS and keeps the lone operation
  but not the first-operation fallback. That is the 1 to 12 m left for ED50 / TM and UTM CRSs,
  Minna / UTM (12.4 m), Aratu, Batavia, Accra and Carthage projected CRSs, and the NAD27 UTM zones
  that need grids. `+datum=NAD27` and NZGD49, which `proj4rs` refuses to build without grid files,
  are covered the same way. A CRS that PROJ transforms only with a ballpark gets no shift, even
  if the bundled table lists a `+towgs84`.
* **Grids.** NTv2 (`.gsb`) and the GeoTIFF grids of PROJ-data (`.tif`: NTv2 and NADCON
  conversions) are read by `proj4rs`. `GeoFrame.Grids` points at the directories holding them
  (`set_dirs/1`, `config :geoframe, grid_dirs: [...]`, or the `PROJ_NADGRIDS` / `PROJ_DATA`
  environment variables, separated by `:`), `status/1` shows which files a CRS would use and
  whether they are installed, and `register/2` overrides the file names for a datum. The file
  names come from the PROJ database (`test/reference/generate_crs_datum.py grid_table`, 32
  geographic CRSs, 104 files, best accuracy first): OSGB36 `uk_os_OSTN15_NTv2_OSGBtoETRS.tif`,
  DHDN `de_adv_BETA2007.tif`, NAD27 `us_noaa_conus.tif`, `ca_nrc_ntv2_0.tif`, ED50
  `es_ign_SPED2ETV2.tif`, NTF, MGI, Tokyo, ... Grids are optional (`+nadgrids=@a,@b`): the ones
  found are used, and a point outside every grid takes the Helmert operation instead of failing,
  as PROJ moves on to its next operation. Grids that shift to NAD83, ETRS89, GDA94 and similar
  count as shifting to WGS 84 (PROJ's own step between them is null, 1 to 2 m).
* **Units, prime meridians and axes (issue #97).** A geographic CRS is read and written in its
  own angular unit: the Paris-meridian CRSs (`EPSG:4807` NTF (Paris), 4901, 4810, 4816, 4821, 4811,
  4902, ...) are in grads, with the Paris meridian applied, as pyproj with `always_xy`. Projected
  CRSs whose WKT points an axis south or west get `+axis` (the table lacked it for `EPSG:5513`
  and `2065`, S-JTSK / Krovak: x is southing, y is westing, so coordinates are positive in the
  Czech Republic; 5514 is the East North twin). `EPSG:3167` has the false easting PROJ has now
  (40 000 chains, the table has 40 000 m).
* **Projections added on top of proj4rs** (`proj_ext.rs`, PROJ's formulas; a CRS using one is
  resolved as the projection plus the `proj4rs` geographic CRS that does the datum shift):
  ellipsoidal Equidistant Cylindrical (`eqc`: `proj4rs` uses a sphere, so `EPSG:4087` was 22 km
  north-south off; PROJ 9 uses the meridian distance), Hotine Oblique Mercator variants A
  (`+no_uoff`) and B (27 codes such as 2057, 3078, 3375), Cassini-Soldner (30 codes: Tobago,
  Berlin, Palestine, ...), Polyconic (5472, 5880, ...), Equal Earth (8857-8859) and the New
  Zealand Map Grid (27200). The `aeqd` feature of `proj4rs` is now on (Guam, FSM: 3295, 3993). Each
  matches pyproj to the centimetre in both directions (fixture `crs_parity.json`). Issue #100
  added Laborde Oblique Mercator (`labrd`, EPSG:8441 and 29701), Hyperbolic Cassini-Soldner
  (3139), Colombia Urban (`col_urban`, 32 codes such as 6244), Krovak Modified (`mod_krovak`:
  5224, 5225, 5515, 5516, with the S-JTSK/05 polynomial) and the spherical Lambert Azimuthal
  Equal Area on the authalic sphere (`+R_A`, EPSG:9311): see below.
* **Accuracy.** With a grid GeoFrame agrees with PROJ to under 1 mm on the same file (a synthetic

## CRS definitions and codes outside the table

Issue #100. `to_crs` and `set_crs` take a CRS as `EPSG:<code>`, `OGC:CRS84`, or a definition:
PROJJSON, WKT1 or WKT2 text, with or without an identifier (as `pyproj.CRS.from_user_input`).

* **Definition converter** (`crs_convert.rs`). PROJJSON (WKT is first translated to PROJJSON:
  `wkt1_projjson.rs` for the OGC WKT1 flavour the bundled table uses, `wkt2_projjson.rs` for
  `GEOGCRS`, `GEODCRS`, `PROJCRS`, `BOUNDCRS` and `COMPOUNDCRS`, datum ensembles included) is turned into the proj
  string the engine runs: ellipsoid (`+a` with `+rf` or `+b`, or `+R`; WKT2 axes in feet are
  converted), prime meridian (`+pm` in degrees), the linear unit (`+to_meter`) and `+axis` when an
  axis points south or west (polar axes named by a meridian are the usual easting and
  northing), a WKT1 `TOWGS84` or the abridged transformation of a bound CRS (`+towgs84`, the
  coordinate frame convention is flipped), and the conversion method, found by its EPSG code or its
  name: Transverse Mercator (also south orientated and 3D), Lambert Conic Conformal (1SP, 1SP
  variant B, 2SP, 2SP Belgium, 2SP Michigan, west orientated), Albers, Lambert Azimuthal Equal
  Area (also spherical), Azimuthal Equidistant (also Modified and Guam), Oblique and Polar
  Stereographic (A, B), Hotine Oblique Mercator (A, B; B with both angles 90 degrees is the
  Swiss `somerc`, as PROJ), Mercator (A, B, spherical, Pseudo-Mercator), Cassini-Soldner (also
  Hyperbolic), Equidistant Cylindrical, Lambert Cylindrical Equal Area, Krovak (North Orientated,
  Modified), Laborde, Colombia Urban, American Polyconic, New Zealand Map Grid and Equal Earth.
  Parameters carry their units (degrees, grads, feet, yards, ...) and are matched by EPSG
  code or EPSG name. A compound CRS uses its horizontal component. Anything else is an error
  naming the method (Bonne, Polar Stereographic variant C, Tunisia Mining Grid, Transverse
  Mercator Zoned Grid System, Local Orthographic).
* **Datum of a definition.** The datum shifts of a CRS (the Helmert operations by area of use
  and the grids) are keyed by the EPSG code of its geographic CRS. A definition that carries that
  identifier (as every definition PROJ or pyproj writes) shifts like the code. One that does
  not finds its geographic CRS by name in `crs_names.rs` (460 names, generated from the PROJ
  database), as PROJ identifies a CRS; a 3D geographic base (LUREF 3D) uses its 2D namesake.
  Failing that it finds it by the name of its **datum** in `crs_datum_names.rs` (1 465 names:
  the EPSG names and the EPSG and ESRI aliases of those 606 datums, `D_OSGB_1936`,
  `D_North_American_1927`, `European_Datum_1950`, `D_GDA_1994`, ...). That is what PROJ does with
  WKT1 text, whatever the name of the GEOGCS: a Shapefile `.prj` (`GCS_...` / `D_...`, no
  authority) is shifted like its EPSG code (GDA94, GDA2020 and CR-SIRGAS included now that their
  null operation is in the table), and a datum name PROJ does not know leaves the text unshifted.
  The datum name is keyed by the longitude of its **prime meridian** (generated from the PROJ
  database with the meridian of each geographic CRS), so `D_MGI` on the Ferro meridian is MGI
  (Ferro) (4805) and not MGI (4312), and NTF on the Paris meridian is NTF (Paris) (4807). A text
  on another meridian answers through that CRS like its EPSG code: Lisbon (Lisbon) 4803, CH1903
  (Bern) 4801, S-JTSK (Ferro) 4818, Batavia (Jakarta) 4813, Segara (Jakarta) 4820, Oslo,
  Tananarive, Carthage (Paris), and the projected CRSs on them (NTF Lambert zones, Austria GK,
  Portuguese grids, NGO 1948, the Jakarta NEIEZ grids, LV03C) agree with pyproj. Two geographic
  texts are refused (`unsupported WKT CRS: the datum ... has a prime meridian at ... degrees and no
  identifier, and PROJ shifts it to WGS 84; GeoFrame's answer for that CRS differs from PROJ's ...`):
  MGI (Ferro), whose answer by code is 305 m off pyproj (PROJ counts the Ferro meridian as
  17.6628 W), and Monte Mario (Rome), which PROJ shifts as the Greenwich datum from text but not by
  code (72 m). Which geographic CRSs those are is generated, not listed: a flag of the table, false
  where the by-code sweep or the text sweep of that geographic CRS differs from pyproj by more than
  5 cm (`CRS_SWEEP_OUT`, `CRS_ESRI_SWEEP_OUT` of `datum_name_table`); a projected CRS on them is
  answered either way. A text whose datum name is known only on another meridian (`D_S_JTSK` is
  Greenwich; S-JTSK (Ferro) East North, EPSG:5221) is refused as well when PROJ shifts that datum.
  A datum name PROJ does not know, or a datum it does not shift (BD50 Brussels, RT38 Stockholm,
  Voirol 1879, the Gusterberg and St. Stephen Ferro systems), keeps its answer. Over 1 337
  geographic and sampled projected CRSs written as WKT1_ESRI (one point each) the differences from
  pyproj were 65 after C1 and are 12 (225 refused on origin/main, 2 now), and over the 5 330
  projected EPSG CRSs (area-of-use centre, one point each) 115 on origin/main and 30 (1 007 refused,
  36 now, 35 of them the projection names listed below); no text that was correct on origin/main is
  wrong or refused now. The 30 left are the ones the CRS has by code too (see the sweep above)
  apart from the two Ferro grids of Gusterberg and St. Stephen (9 and 4 m: the Cassini-Soldner
  series of `proj4rs` 1.4 degrees from the central meridian). ATF (Paris) as geographic text, in
  grads, is 178 km off (its unit, not its datum) and Tokyo 1892 and Rassadiran differ for their own
  PROJ-specific operations: documented deviations, not pursued.
  ESRI names several
  projections differently from EPSG, and these are read as the EPSG method PROJ maps them to:
  `Double_Stereographic` (Oblique Stereographic, RD New), `Gauss_Kruger` (Transverse Mercator),
  `Albers` (Albers Equal Area), `Cassini`, `Stereographic_North_Pole` / `Stereographic_South_Pole`
  (Polar Stereographic variant B), `Stereographic` / `Polar_Stereographic_Variant_A` on a pole
  (variant A), `Mercator` with a standard parallel (variant B), `Hotine_Oblique_Mercator_Azimuth_Natural_Origin`
  and `Rectified_Skew_Orthomorphic_Natural_Origin` (Hotine variant A), `Rectified_Skew_Orthomorphic_Center`
  (variant B), `Equidistant_Cylindrical`, `Behrmann`, `Equal_Earth`, ESRI's `Krovak` (south
  orientated, or north orientated with `X_Scale` -1 and `XY_Plane_Rotation` 90), a two-parallel
  `Lambert_Conformal_Conic` with a scale factor (2SP Michigan), and the EPSG parameter names
  PROJ writes for some of them (`Transverse_Mercator_South_Orientated`, `Lambert_Azimuthal_Equal_Area`,
  `Azimuthal_Equidistant`, `Lambert_Conformal_Conic_2SP_Belgium`, `New_Zealand_Map_Grid`);
  `Hotine_Oblique_Mercator_Azimuth_*` without a rectified grid angle use the azimuth, as PROJ does.
  Still refused: `IGAC_Plano_Cartesiano` (Colombia Urban; proj4rs differs from PROJ by about
  1 m there, also for EPSG:6244), `Local`, `Cylindrical_Equal_Area` with EPSG parameter names (PROJ
  ignores the standard parallel), a non-polar `Stereographic`, and `Transverse_Mercator`
  with `Direction` / `Vertical_Shift`. Over the 5 330 projected EPSG CRSs written as WKT1_ESRI
  (area-of-use centre, one point each) 1 007 were refused before and 35 are now; the CRSs that
  newly differ from pyproj (26) are the datum deviations above (Ferro, Jakarta, Bern, RD/83,
  Potsdam, Qatar 1948, ...), not the projection.
  Any other datum is left unshifted, which is PROJ's ballpark and what pyproj does for a custom
  datum.
* **Labels.** A definition with an identifier that the tables have is that code (`EPSG:5515`). Any
  other definition is its own label: the canonical JSON text (or the WKT text as given), which
  resolves again to the same CRS, so `set_crs`, `to_crs`, `crs_equivalent` and the
  compatibility checks of joins and overlays work on it. `crs_to_authority` (GeoParquet) and
  Shapefile `.prj` / GeoPackage use the identifier or the WKT text.
* **Codes outside the `crs-definitions` table.** The table is older than the registry: 851
  non-deprecated geographic 2D and projected codes were missing (568 Transverse Mercator, 143
  geographic, 41 Lambert conic 2SP, 32 Colombia Urban, 24 Lambert conic 1SP, ...), and it lists
  neither 3D geographic CRSs nor, for the most part, geocentric or compound ones. A supplementary table,
  `crs_extra.bin`, holds the PROJJSON of every non-deprecated EPSG geographic (2D and 3D),
  geocentric, projected and compound CRS the table lacks, and of the two entries it leaves
  without a proj string (22300, 29701): 1 217 CRSs, 1.55 MB of minified JSON without the text
  fields (scope, area, remarks, bbox), 51 KB deflate-compressed (`flate2` is already in the
  tree through `zip` and `parquet`). It is generated from proj.db by
  `generate_crs_datum.py extra_table`, inflated on first use, read by the converter like user
  PROJJSON and written as full PROJJSON to GeoParquet (pyproj reads it). The release NIF
  (`libgeoframe_native.so`, unstripped) grows from 38 394 160 to 38 662 976 bytes (+269 KB, 0.7 %):
  the 51 KB table, the larger Helmert and name tables, the converter and the new projections. The data are the EPSG dataset (see `NOTICE` for the attribution
  and terms of use). WKT1 for Shapefile `.prj` and GeoPackage is still only available for the
  codes of the `crs-definitions` table.
* **Tests.** `crs_convert.json` (`generate_crs_datum.py convert`): 42 CRSs as PROJJSON without an
  identifier, 12 as WKT1, 13 as WKT2, and 4 codes from the supplementary table, against pyproj
  within 1 cm (Laborde 5 cm, Hyperbolic Cassini-Soldner inverse 10 cm: PROJ evaluates series
  there); the whole registry through the sweep above.

* **Not done.** Vertical or geoid grids and 3D shifts (heights stay unchanged); `NADCON`
  `.las/.los` text grids and NTv1 (only the `.tif` conversions and NTv2 are read);
  `pyproj.sync`-style downloads and `PROJ_NETWORK`; pipelines; operations through an intermediate
  CRS that change the prime meridian between Helmert steps (Tokyo 1892, MGI (Ferro)); PROJ's
  area test in the projected coordinates (a point just outside the geographic area of use);
  Local Orthographic (EPSG:10622), Bonne, Polar Stereographic C, Tunisia Mining Grid and
  Transverse Mercator Zoned Grid System as definitions; WKT1 for the codes only in the
  supplementary table.

- Z2 status: done. The geo bridge now has a Z/M-carrying path (`zm_bridge.rs`). *Vertex maps* rewrite the ISO WKB of a row in place, so `(x, y)` is transformed and Z and M pass through or are transformed: the affine family and `to_crs`. *Restoring* runs the 2D algorithm and gives every surviving vertex the Z (and M) of the input vertex with exactly the same XY (first occurrence; NaN when none): `simplify`, `normalize`, `boundary`, `exterior`, `get_geometry`, `explode`, `set_precision`, `snap`. `reverse` reverses the WKB coordinate sequences. 2D columns do not pay for any of this. 1 154 reference cases in `zm_ops.json` (902 agree, 252 are documented) compare the result with GeoPandas as full WKT.
- Policy per operation (observed in Shapely 2.2 / GEOS 3.14): Z kept and M dropped by `translate` (`zoff`), `scale` (`zfact`, about z = 0), `rotate`, `skew`, `affine_transform` (the 12-number form transforms Z; a 2D geometry only uses `a b d e xoff yoff`), `to_crs` (Z unchanged between non-geocentric CRSs, as PROJ; transformed to or from a geocentric CRS), `simplify`, `set_precision(mode: :pointwise)` and `snap` (the snapped vertex takes the reference vertex's Z, NaN when the reference has none; a vertex that coincides with a reference vertex takes its Z too). Z and M kept by `reverse`, `normalize`, `boundary`, `exterior`, `interiors`, `get_geometry`, `explode` and `set_precision` in the `:valid_output` and `:keep_collapsed` modes. Every other operation (`envelope`, `centroid`, `buffer`, `offset_curve`, `minimum_rotated_rectangle`, `shortest_line`, ...) returns 2D, where GEOS drops Z too. - Z3 status: done (`zm_overlay.rs`, 19 unit tests in `zm_overlay_tests.rs`, 784 reference cases in `zm_overlay.json`: 744 agree, 40 are the documented deviations below). The 2D result is computed by `geo` as before and every vertex then gets its ordinates from a model of GEOS' `OverlayNG` built from the input rows: (1) a vertex that equals an input vertex takes its Z (and M); (2) a vertex on an input segment interpolates along it, and a crossing averages the interpolation on the two segments (a NaN end hands back the other end, as `LineIntersector::zInterpolate`); (3) Z that is still NaN is filled from the GEOS `ElevationModel` (the inputs' extent cut in 3x3 cells holding the mean Z of their vertices, an empty cell taking the mean of the others); M is interpolated the same way but never filled. Vertices `geo` moved by its output grid (about 1e-9 of the extent) are matched to the nearest input vertex within 1e-7 of the extent. Rows without Z and M take the old path, so 2D columns pay nothing.
- Z3 policy found (Shapely 2.2 / GEOS 3.14): Z interpolated, M dropped by `segmentize` (a new vertex interpolates between the two input vertices it was inserted between, also where segments cross), `interpolate` and `clip_by_rect` (a row inside the rectangle comes back unchanged, M included; an empty result is 2D; corners GEOS adds inside a polygon take the elevation-model value). Z kept, M dropped by `convex_hull`, `concave_hull`, `extract_unique_points`, `line_merge`, `delaunay_triangles`, `polygonize`, `build_area` and `representative_point` of a line (a polygon or point gives a new 2D point). Z and M kept by `remove_repeated_points` and `orient_polygons`; `make_valid` keeps both for a valid geometry and Z only (with the averaged Z at new nodes) for a repaired one. Set operations, `union_all`, `intersection_all`, `overlay`, `clip` and `dissolve` follow `OverlayNG`: the result has Z and M when either input has them (M interpolated, NaN where an input has none). Points are not noded: they keep their own Z or take the elevation-model value (a point on a Z line is not interpolated). Inputs with disjoint extents are copied: a union leaves a 2D input NaN, a difference fills it. An empty result has Z when either input has a third ordinate after overlaying and only when both have one for disjoint extents, never M. 2D in GEOS and here: `project`/`line_locate_point` (a distance), `centroid`, `envelope`, `buffer`, `offset_curve`, `minimum_rotated_rectangle`, `minimum_bounding_circle`, `minimum_clearance_line`, `shortest_line`, `voronoi_polygons`.

(16) The 2D differences already documented (line x line noding, polygon boundaries not noded by a line, empty point results as `MULTIPOINT`) are unchanged.

- Z4 policy for mixed dimensions: rows of a column may differ in dimension everywhere a format can say so per row (GeoJSON, GeoPackage, GeoParquet/Arrow WKB). Formats with one dimension per file promote to the widest with NaN (FlatGeobuf: Z, M or both; Shapefile: a 2D row in a Z file writes a NaN Z, where GDAL writes 0). ## Resource limits

Inputs that would abort the whole VM (stack overflow or a huge allocation) are rejected with an
error (`{:error, message}` or `ArgumentError`) instead. The caps:

| Area | Limit |
|------|-------|
| WKT text | parenthesis nesting depth 64 |
| WKB / EWKB | collection nesting depth 64 (the `wkb` reader itself stops at 128) |
| FlatGeobuf read | every feature must fit in the file; `bbox:` reads need index node size 16; a header that declares Z or M requires one value per vertex in every feature |
| Shapefile read (Z/M) | the part and point counts of a Z or M record must fit the record, so a crafted count is an error, not an allocation |
| `make_grid` | 10,000,000 cells (square and hexagon) |
| `sample_points` | 10,000,000 samples in total per call (a scalar size counts once per row) |
| `maximum_inscribed_circle` | seed cells are enlarged until at most 5,000,000 fit; refinement is also capped at 5,000,000 cells |
| `segmentize` | 10,000,000 estimated output vertices |
| `hausdorff_distance` / `frechet_distance` `densify:` | fraction at least `1.0e-4` (10,000 pieces per segment) and 10,000,000 densified vertices in total |
| `union_groups` | `n_groups` at most the number of ids; ids below `n_groups` |
| `offset_curve` | `quad_segs` at most 10,000; an estimated 10,000,000 raw vertices (vertices times `2 * quad_segs`) is an error; a geometry whose cutting needs over 50,000,000 segment pairs is `nil`; non-finite distance or coordinates give `nil` |
| `snap` | vertices times snap points at most 50,000,000 per geometry pair (a row over it is `nil`) |
| `polygonize` / `build_area` | the noding examines at most 50,000,000 candidate segment pairs (an error beyond that) |
| `is_valid_coverage` / `invalid_coverage_edges` | candidate segment pairs plus point-in-polygon steps at most 50,000,000 per call (an error beyond that); `gap_width` must be finite; rows with non-finite coordinates are `nil` |
| `simplify_coverage` | at most 10,000,000 vertices and 50,000,000 vertex-in-triangle checks (an error beyond that); `tolerance` must be finite; rows with non-finite coordinates are `nil` |

Writers (GeoParquet, Feather, GeoJSON, FlatGeobuf) write to `<path>.tmp-<id>` beside the
destination and rename it over `path` on success; a failure never removes an existing file.
CRS labels that differ only by case, or `OGC:CRS84` against `EPSG:4326`, are compatible for
binary operations, joins, overlay and `concat`.

`to_crs` / `set_crs` take any EPSG geographic, geocentric, projected or compound CRS (the
`crs-definitions` table plus a 51 KB supplementary table generated from the PROJ database), or a
PROJJSON or WKT (WKT1, WKT2) definition without a code, as `pyproj.CRS.from_user_input` does.
A definition is converted for the common projection methods; any other method is an error that
names it. See [CRS definitions and codes outside the table](#crs-definitions-and-codes-outside-the-table).

Shapefile and zip input/output:

- Zip archives (zipped Shapefiles): a member may decompress to at most 2 GiB (`MAX_ZIP_MEMBER_BYTES`); larger members are an error.
- dBase tables (`.dbf`): a table may expand to at most 2^28 cells (records times fields), and its header must be consistent
  (record length covers the fields, header length covers the descriptors); inconsistent or amplifying headers are an error.
- Shapefile sidecars are named after the path: a path ending in `.shp` swaps that suffix, any other path gets the extension
  appended to its full name (`out.v1` writes `out.v1.shp`, `out.v1.dbf`, ...). Shapefile and zip writes go through temporary
  files and renames, so a failed write leaves existing files as they were.
